Compact Paddle Launcher

Paddle launcher v1, compact build

DWG PL-03 · FRONT VIEW + SIDE SECTION · MM · NEMA 23 + 10:1 WORM

The layout: NEMA 23 into a 10:1 worm gearbox, spring around the output shaft, paddle on the end. A drive arm keyed to the shaft drags the paddle back with a hook. A servo opens the hook to fire. The paddle hub turns on its own bushing, so once the hook lets go nothing connects the swing to the gearbox. Top setting is 20 m/s at the tip.

Side section along the output shaft

Seen from the right, the front of the machine to the right. Everything sits on the gearbox's own output shaft. Only the drive arm is keyed to it. The paddle hub turns on a bronze bushing, and the spring's coil just rides on the shaft.

Acceleration settings

Average acceleration is end speed² ÷ (2 × cock angle). The paddle turns 15° before its pin picks up the spring leg, which keeps the low settings gentle. The spring is preloaded to 11.5 N·m on its leg stop and rises 0.0996 N·m per degree after pickup. Paddle 0.022 kg·m²; paddle plus hub 0.0228 kg·m². Motor torque assumes 85% gearbox efficiency.

Firmware cycle

Strength is only the number of motor turns in step 1: 10 motor turns per output turn, so 44.4 steps per degree at 1600 steps per motor turn.

Core of the sketch

// AccelStepper + ESP32Servo. 1600 steps per motor turn, 10:1 worm
const float STEPS_PER_DEG = 1600.0 * 10 / 360;   // 44.4
float cockDeg(long accel);           // from the settings table

void shoot(long accel) {
  hook.write(LOCK);
  stepper.setMaxSpeed(8000);         // 300 rpm
  stepper.moveTo(-cockDeg(accel) * STEPS_PER_DEG);
  while (stepper.distanceToGo()) {
    if (stepper.currentPosition() < -95 * STEPS_PER_DEG)
      stepper.setMaxSpeed(1600);     // 60 rpm near the top
    stepper.run();
  }
  waitForFire();                     // driver stays enabled
  hook.write(RELEASE);  delay(300);  // paddle has swung
  stepper.setMaxSpeed(8000);
  stepper.moveTo(0);  stepper.runToPosition();  // arm back, hook snaps over
  hook.write(LOCK);
}

Build steps

  1. Gearbox. Bolt the gearbox to the board with the output shaft pointing forward and the motor hanging below. Fit the long keyed output shaft so about 140 mm sticks out of the front.
  2. Spring. Slide it onto the shaft against the gearbox face. Put its rear leg against the anchor post. Wind the front leg about 116° with a bar and drop it behind the leg-stop post. It now holds 11.5 N·m on its own, so keep fingers clear.
  3. Drive arm. Bolt the arm to the keyed flange, then key and set-screw the flange onto the shaft just in front of the spring. Mount the hook on its pivot, the servo beside the hook's tail, and a light spring that pulls the hook closed.
  4. Paddle hub. Press the two bushings into the 1 in thick aluminum block and slide it on the shaft end. Hold it with a washer and a bolt into the shaft's end. The M10 pin goes through the block 48 mm below the centre: its roller lines up with the hook, and its rear end reaches back to the spring's front leg.
  5. Paddle and absorber. Clamp the handle on the hub with two hose clamps. Bolt the absorber's bracket to the board so its cap just touches the hub's stop tab with the paddle vertical.

Calibrate, limits, safety

  • 1 · Find the pickup. Put a strip of paper between the pin and the spring leg. Jog the cock until the paper pinches, and store that angle as the slack (designed at 15°). At 7,000 °/s², 0.1° of error moves the shot about 3%.
  • 2 · Check the top. Fire at the top setting and film it at 240 fps, or use a speed sensor. If the tip misses 20 m/s, scale the firmware's spring rate by (measured ÷ 20)².
  • Motor. A top-setting cock needs 22 N·m at the shaft, which is about 2.7 N·m at the motor. Use a NEMA 23 rated about 3 N·m holding, and finish the cock at 60 rpm. The spring's preload means a weaker motor loses a lot: a 1.9 N·m motor would stall at about 10 m/s.
  • Spring. It is the one part to buy carefully. Look for 0.88 in-lb per degree, rated for at least 240° of deflection and 200 in-lb, left-hand wound, coil ID over 20 mm.
  • Absorber. The top shot puts about 28 J into it, arriving at 3.3 m/s, inside an AC2525's rating.
  • Safety. The spring holds 11.5 N·m even at rest, and about 42 J at the top setting. Keep the driver enabled while cocked: if power drops, the paddle stays hooked and unwinds back through the gearbox, so stand clear. Never fire without the paddle on.

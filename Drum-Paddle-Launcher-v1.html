Drum Paddle Launcher

Paddle launcher v1, option 2: drum and extension springs

DWG PL-02 · PLAN + SIDE VIEW · MM · PADDLE SWINGS FLAT

Two extension springs lie flat behind the shaft and pull a cord wrapped on a 5 in V-pulley. The pulley is bolted into the paddle hub. The release is the same as in the compact launcher: the drive arm keyed to the gearbox drags the hub back with a hook, and a servo opens the hook to fire. Item numbers match the option 2 bill of materials in the v1 PDF.

The roller (8) on the hook (7) pushes on the lug (13), which is bolted to the bottom of the paddle hub. To fire, the servo (6) swings its horn off the hook's tail, and the lug shoves the roller out of the way. On reset, the hook's ramped nose rides over the lug and snaps shut.

One shot, step by step

The same sequence the buttons above play.

At rest

The springs are already stretched to 498 mm (30 lb each), but the equalizer sits on the spring stop, so none of that reaches the paddle. The cord has 10 mm of slack.

Cock

The motor turns the drive arm clockwise and the hook drags the hub with it. After 10° the cord goes tight. From there the pulley winds it in, lifting the equalizer off the stop and stretching both springs.

Hold

The worm and the powered motor hold the arm; the hook holds the hub. At 120° each spring pulls about 41 lb and the equalizer has moved about 106 mm forward.

Fire

The servo swings its horn off the hook. The lug knocks the hook open and the springs pull the cord, which spins the pulley and the paddle counterclockwise. The arm and gearbox don't move.

Stop and reset

10° before rest the equalizer lands on the spring stop and the cord goes slack. The paddle coasts through rest, and the absorber stops it about 20° later. The motor then brings the arm back and the hook snaps over the lug.

Settings

Same numbers as the v1 PDF. Steps assume 1600 steps per motor turn and 10:1, so 44.4 steps per degree. Paddle 0.022 kg·m², hub 0.0029 kg·m², springs 2 × 2.7 lb/in on a 55 mm cord radius, preload 14.7 N·m held by the spring stop, 10° of slack.
